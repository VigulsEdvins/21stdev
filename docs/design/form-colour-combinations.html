Colour combinations: whole page, neighbouring sections, booking form

Generated by npm run preview:palettes from 40 templates. For each template: the page as bands from top to bottom (60-30-10 shares, every pair of neighbouring bands judged, the colours each band may take between its neighbours), then the 246 combinations the booking form may take; the page picks the first one by itself (scripts/preview/form-palette.browser.js).

Rules

  • Monochromatic (one hue, different lightness) is the safest harmony; analogous hues sit 30–60° apart on the colour wheel and feel calm.
  • Complementary (180°), split-complementary (the two hues beside the complement) and triadic (120°) give strong contrast: use them as the accent, not as large areas; two saturated complements in equal amounts vibrate.
  • 60-30-10: dominant ~60% (the page), secondary ~30% (the form panel), accent ~10% (button, progress, ticks).
  • So the panel only uses a colour the template already uses, or a deeper / lighter tone of one; never a foreign colour (the charcoal next to the plum page). It must differ from the bands right above and below it; a tone of the band above is preferred, so the page flows.
  • Neighbouring sections: two bands of almost the same colour look like a mistake (make them one colour or clearly different); two large areas in contrasting hues compete, so one of them should become an accent; a light-to-dark change is a clear, intended break.
  • Readability is separate from harmony (WCAG): text on the panel 7:1, accent against the panel 3:1, text on the button 4.5:1.

Sources: Inventive HQ, colour harmonies, Readymag, colour combinations, ColorFYI, 60-30-10, Penpot, web colour theory.
